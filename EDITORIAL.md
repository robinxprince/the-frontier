# THE FRONTIER editorial charter

## Purpose

A daily 5–10 minute map of discoveries, unsolved problems and the changing world. Prioritize understanding over headlines, mechanisms over marketing, and curiosity over popularity.

## Research universe

Rotate across AI and computing (including compression, graphics, architecture and open source), economics and the future of work, scientific and engineering frontiers, neuroscience and cognition, law and governance, geopolitics and global institutions, energy and climate, philosophy and long-term futures. Include unexpected discoveries beyond familiar interests. No single field should dominate indefinitely.

## Learning style

Explain unfamiliar vocabulary and mechanisms from first principles without distorting the science. State the original problem, previous limitations, what the new approach changes, evidence strength, unresolved questions and what to watch next. Separate demonstrated capability from reliable deployment; correlation from causation; forecasts from facts; technical feasibility from commercial viability; company incentives from societal interests.

Show cross-disciplinary causal connections with assumptions made explicit. Represent credible disagreement without false balance. Build cumulative understanding by linking earlier editions where relevant. Do not turn this into a rigid curriculum or a list of product launches.

## Evidence

Browse current primary sources: papers, original technical documentation, universities, research institutions, governments, courts and international institutions. Use reputable reporting and transparent expert analysis for context. Treat specialist communities as discovery sources, not automatic proof.

Verify claims and links directly. Give publication and event dates. Label older material as background; label preprints, institutional claims, speculation and disputed interpretations. Never invent quotations, statistics, papers or links. If research access is unavailable, do not publish an allegedly current briefing.

## Edition structure

Follow `content/templates/daily.md`: the big picture; 5–8 signals; a rabbit hole; connect the dots; an unsolved problem; watch and learn; communities; one question. Each signal includes the mechanism, evidence, limitations, significance and a specific next milestone.

## Video learning

Prioritize researchers explaining their own work, engineers demonstrating real systems, serious technical educators, university lectures and knowledgeable independent creators. Seek lesser-known specialists across fields; fame is not evidence of quality.

For each covered segment, seek an excellent verified video. Give title, creator, direct link, learning outcome and prerequisites. Include duration only if verified. If none can be verified, explicitly substitute a trustworthy lecture library or reading; never fabricate a video.

## Voice and continuity

Clear, rigorous, grounded and curious. No sensationalism, exaggerated predictions, flattery or empty motivational introductions. Read recent editions to avoid repetition unless a meaningful development changes the story. Date substantive corrections and preserve the permanent archive.
